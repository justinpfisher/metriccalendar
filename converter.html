A transparent calculation

Count the day.
Keep the year.

Both calendars begin the year together and have the same number of days. Conversion changes the month and day label, preserving the ordinal day (its position in the year).

  1. 01

    Find the day of year

    Add the lengths of the completed months to the day of the month in the input calendar. January 1 is day 1.

  2. 02

    Use the other month lengths

    Subtract complete months in the destination calendar until the remaining count fits inside a month.

  3. 03

    Read the new date

    The remainder is the day of that month. The year stays the same. For Metric dates, classify the day using the six-day pattern or extra-day rules.

Metric month lengths are 36, 37, 36, 37, 36, 37, 36, 37, 36, 37; the final value becomes 38 in a leap year. See the full month table and leap-year rule.

An ordinary date

July 1, 2026

Gregorian day of year:
31 + 28 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 30 + 1 = 182.

The first four metric months total 36 + 37 + 36 + 37 = 146 days. The remainder is 182 − 146 = 36.

g2026-07-01 = 2026-05-36

A Bonus Rest Day

March 14, 2026

Gregorian day of year:
31 + 28 + 14 = 73.

Subtract the first metric month: 73 − 36 = 37. Day 37 of month 02 is outside the metric week.

g2026-03-14 = 2026-02-37

A note on old dates and recurring events

The converter uses Gregorian rules throughout years 1–9999, not historical local calendars. “Today” uses your device’s local date. For a recurring Gregorian birthday or holiday, convert each occurrence in its own year: after February, leap years can change the Metric month/day. Read the practical guidance.
