The idea in a minute

A familiar year.
A new arrangement.

The length of the day stays the same. So do the year number, January 1 alignment, and leap-year rule. Here is the proposed structure.

10months

A repeatable month

Each month has 36 regular days. Even-numbered months have a 37th day; month 10 has a 38th in leap years.

4focus + 2 rest

A six-day metric week

Four Focus Days followed by two Rest Days, repeated six times per month. These labels describe the proposed rhythm.

5bonus days

Space between cycles

A Bonus Rest Day closes months 02, 04, 06, 08, and 10, outside the regular metric week. No day disappears.

Why explore it?

A simpler pattern.
Some bigger questions.

Regular month layouts make the calendar easier to describe. Whether that makes life easier is a question for people, organisations, and research.

01

Learn a predictable structure

Every month begins with Focus Day 1. The six regular metric weeks have the same layout.

02

Try a planning overlay

Keep Gregorian dates alongside Metric dates to explore a different view of your year.

03

Examine the trade-offs

A six-day cycle shifts against the seven-day week. Family schedules, observances, and services need deliberate attention.

A proposal, with open questions

The arithmetic is defined.
The benefits need testing.

Studies of shorter working time provide useful context. They do not test this calendar’s ten months or its six-day metric week, and they do not guarantee productivity or well-being gains.
