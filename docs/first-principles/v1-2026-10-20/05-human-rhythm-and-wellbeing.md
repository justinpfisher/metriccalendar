# 5 — Human rhythm and well-being: independent second-stage investigation

**This investigation follows the mathematical selection in documents 1–4. It does not change that selection.** OSC1's mathematically necessary cycle is only the year; it implies **no shorter work/rest week**. Human rhythm is a separately specifiable behaviour, employment policy and cultural practice.

## Structural rhythms available

- **Day:** a real physical/biological day is unchanged. Sleep–wake timing and circadian entrainment remain anchored to environmental light/dark and individual physiology, not calendar numbers.
- **Year:** a chosen seasonal reference and epoch. Yearly festivals, evaluation and planning can align with seasons, but even a 91-day quarter is not an exactly equal astronomical season.
- **Optional divisional views:** 4×91+1(+L), 5×73(+L), 13×28+1(+L), continuous seven-day weeks, continuous five- or six-day schedules, rolling 10-day review cycles. None must control the date identity.
- **Year-end ordinal 365 (and 366 in leap years):** offers a natural **symbolic transition opportunity**, but is not mathematically required to be a holiday, outside a week or outside a person's work rota.
- **No mandatory weekly interruption:** an independent schedule position can be computed as 1+((absolute_day−anchor) mod w), continuing through every date.

## What credible empirical research supports

**Reduced time at work can improve some well-being outcomes in participating organisations.** Fan, Schor, Kelly and Gu (Nature Human Behaviour, 2025) studied an organisation-wide four-day, income-preserving working-time intervention with work redesign and six-month follow-up. The report covers **2,896 employees in 141 organisations**, with **12 control companies**, and reports improvements in burnout, job satisfaction, mental health and physical health, associated with reduced sleep problems and fatigue. These are valuable findings; the study is not a global random assignment of employees to all conceivable work patterns, and process redesign is part of the treatment. It does **not** establish how a four-in-six schedule performs. [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40691306/).

**The UK six-month trial** involved 61 participating organisations and an intended 20% reduction in hours without lowering pay. Cambridge reported **71%** of respondents reporting lower burnout, **39%** reporting lower stress, a **65%** reduction in sick days and **57%** less staff turnover compared with the preceding year; the company sample with revenue data was smaller and revenue barely changed on average. Participation, selection, no general randomisation, before/after confounding and heterogeneous industry mix limit generalisation. [Cambridge report](https://www.cam.ac.uk/stories/fourdayweek).

**The Iceland 2015–2019 trials** reduced public-sector schedules to ~35–36 hours for roughly 2,500 employees; the trial authors report maintained or improved productivity/service at most workplaces and enhanced well-being. This is evidence about particular negotiated shorter working hours, not about replacing a calendar. [Alda and Autonomy](https://en.alda.is/2021/07/04/going-public-icelands-journey-to-a-shorter-working-week/).

**Long hours are associated with health risks.** WHO/ILO systematic evidence finds a higher risk of ischaemic heart disease and stroke at **55+ hours/week** compared with **35–40 hours/week**. This argues for measuring *actual exposure* rather than seeing "Rest Day" in a calendar cell. It does not establish one universal ideal daily or weekly schedule. [WHO/ILO](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/global-regional-and-national-burdens-of-ischemic-heart-disease-and-stroke-attributable-to-exposure-to-long-working-hours-for-194-countries-2000-2016).

**Recovery can be valuable, but duration and timing matter.** A 2025 meta-analysis of 32 studies found positive associations between vacation and employee well-being and explored psychological detachment and activities during leave; it does not specify how many off-calendar days a solar year should contain. An earlier meta-analysis of seven studies found benefits that faded after returning to work, underscoring heterogeneity and measurement-design limits. [2025 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39836131/); [earlier study](https://academic.oup.com/joh/article/51/1/13/7270123).

**Compressed workweeks are not equivalent to reduced hours.** A 2025 systematic review of **20 longitudinal studies** found mixed health and performance outcomes from compressed schedules (frequently extended shifts). Evidence was more concerning for some 12-hour arrangements; satisfaction could improve despite negative health markers. A four-day week made of four 10- or 12-hour shifts is not the same intervention as four eight-hour shifts. [Systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12484334/).

## Seven-day biology: careful interpretation

Circadian (~24-hour) organisation is strongly connected to solar day/night cues. Research describes *circaseptan* (~7-day) signatures in some biological outcomes, including evidence from controlled environments, but the origin, strength, independence from social timing, and optimal practical rest cycle remain debated. A 2016 narrative review itself presents endogenous explanations and repair-time functions as **hypotheses** and highlights unknown mechanisms. It is unwarranted to claim that either **exactly six days**, **exactly seven days**, or **four Focus Days** is biologically mandated. [Review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27830946/); [historical biological investigation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7111982/).

## Candidate activity/rest rhythms (not calendar rules)

| Repeating schedule | Typical continuous cycle | Recovery pattern | Principal uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Five on / two off over seven | 40 hours in 7 days at 8/day | 2 consecutive days | Might be less recovery than preferred for some, but embedded social coordination |
| Four on / three off over seven | 32 hours in 7 days at 8/day | 3 consecutive days | Requires work reorganisation and more coverage in many services |
| Four on / two off over six | 32 hours in 6 days → 37⅓ hours per 7 days | 2 consecutive days more frequently | No comparable direct large-scale controlled studies of six-day rotation |
| Three on / two off over five | 24 hours in 5 days → 33.6 hours per 7 days | 2 consecutive days more frequently | Unfamiliar social cycle and untested biological fit |
| Flexible hours, flexible rest | Variable | Adapted to care, shift safety and preference | Need clear minimum recovery, fairness and coordination rules |

A mathematically perfect recurring rest pattern can still cause stress if spouses, families, schools, carers and friends have incompatible schedules. Social synchronisation is a candidate **mediator**, not peripheral "resistance to change."

## Recommendation

**Use a neutral mathematical calendar plus a separate, optional human-application layer** — category (3) from the brief, with optional recommended defaults for the relevant setting. For eligible trial workplaces, investigate a **genuine reduction in paid hours** (for example, four eight-hour days in a continuous seven-day cycle) with no automatic reduction in salary, optional role-specific scheduling and protections against compressing hours into fatigue-producing shifts. Other contexts may justifiably retain 5/2, rotating shifts, shorter individual shifts or asynchronous flexibility.

### Minimum credible future evaluation

Pre-register hypotheses, control/comparison groups where feasible, baseline and follow-up. Capture self-reported life satisfaction and recovery; measured sleep; exhaustion/burnout; family conflict and leisure/social connection; scheduled and actual hours, overtime and unpaid care; quality, errors and service coverage; staffing costs and retention. Analyse by occupation and caring responsibilities. Exclude a compulsory global week change from the initial test. Record adverse and neutral findings and enable opt-out.

**No claim is supported that merely changing calendar numbers increases happiness.** The most promising mechanisms come from working-time and organisational choices that could also be made using the existing Gregorian dates.
