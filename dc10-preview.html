Ten months. Six-day weeks. A different year.

Try the mathematically balanced research calendar: ten months of 36 or 37 days, each with six regular weeks, four Focus Days and two Rest Days. Every long month ends with an additional Rest Day.

The proposed structure

Each month has six complete six-day weeks. The weekday names are Begin, Create, Connect, Share, Pause, Renew; days 1–4 are Focus Days and days 5–6 are Rest Days. A month with Day 37 adds a Bonus Rest Day outside the regular weekly positions.

Annual arithmetic: 60 full weeks, 240 Focus Days, 120 regular Rest Days and five or six additional Rest Days. The leap addition is Month 05 Day 37, counted once. Month 10 always ends at Day 37.

Why this is not yet the adopted calendar

The provisional year-start rule is 31 leap years in 128, anchored to 20 March 2000 UTC. A check against published equinox dates for 2000–2099 found a mismatch in 2073; longer-range astronomical testing remains unfinished. The six-day Focus/Rest classification is a chosen calendar convention, not a scientifically proven schedule for maximum happiness or productivity.

Clocks, time zones, physical day lengths, Gregorian legal dates and the current published specification remain unchanged. See the astronomy and optional decimal-time investigation.
